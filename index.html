💧 Water Parameter Guidance

1. Water pH

Measures whether water is acidic, neutral or alkaline.

  • Below 6.5: Acidic
  • 6.5–8.5: General recommended range
  • Above 8.5: Alkaline

2. Total Hardness

Measures dissolved calcium and magnesium in water.

  • 0–60 mg/L: Soft
  • 61–120 mg/L: Moderately hard
  • 121–200 mg/L: Hard
  • Above 200 mg/L: Very hard

3. Turbidity

Measures the cloudiness of water caused by suspended particles.

  • Below 1 NTU: Low turbidity
  • 1–5 NTU: Moderate turbidity
  • Above 5 NTU: High turbidity

4. Total Dissolved Solids (TDS)

Measures dissolved substances in water.

  • Below 300 mg/L: Generally low
  • 300–500 mg/L: Generally desirable
  • 501–1000 mg/L: Elevated
  • Above 1000 mg/L: High

5. Chloride

Dissolved chloride salts; high levels give a salty taste and corrode pipes.

  • Up to 250 mg/L: Acceptable
  • 251–1000 mg/L: Permissible only if no alternative
  • Above 1000 mg/L: Not suitable

6. Sulphate

Comes from minerals and waste; high levels taste bitter and can act as a laxative.

  • Up to 200 mg/L: Acceptable
  • 201–400 mg/L: Permissible only if no alternative
  • Above 400 mg/L: Not suitable

7. Nitrate

Usually from fertiliser, sewage or septic leakage; harmful to infants.

  • Up to 45 mg/L: Acceptable
  • Above 45 mg/L: Not suitable (no relaxation)

8. Fluoride

Beneficial in small amounts, harmful in excess (fluorosis).

  • Up to 1.0 mg/L: Acceptable
  • 1.1–1.5 mg/L: Permissible only if no alternative
  • Above 1.5 mg/L: Not suitable

9. Total Alkalinity

Ability of water to neutralise acid, mainly from bicarbonates and carbonates.

  • Up to 200 mg/L as CaCO₃: Acceptable
  • 201–600 mg/L: Permissible only if no alternative
  • Above 600 mg/L: Not suitable

10. Calcium

Major hardness-causing mineral.

  • Up to 75 mg/L: Acceptable
  • 76–200 mg/L: Permissible only if no alternative
  • Above 200 mg/L: Not suitable

11. Magnesium

Second hardness-causing mineral; high levels taste bitter.

  • Up to 30 mg/L: Acceptable
  • 31–100 mg/L: Permissible only if no alternative
  • Above 100 mg/L: Not suitable

12. Iron

Common in groundwater; causes stains and a metallic taste.

  • Up to 0.3 mg/L: Acceptable
  • Above 0.3 mg/L: Not suitable (no relaxation)

13. Free Residual Chlorine

Chlorine left after disinfection that keeps water protected from germs.

  • Below 0.2 mg/L: Too low for reliable disinfection
  • 0.2–1.0 mg/L: Recommended range
  • Above 1.0 mg/L: Higher than usual; above 4 mg/L is too high

14. Ammonia

Signal of sewage or organic pollution.

  • Up to 0.5 mg/L: Acceptable
  • Above 0.5 mg/L: Possible contamination

15. Arsenic

A toxic element found naturally in some groundwater.

  • Up to 0.01 mg/L: Acceptable
  • 0.011–0.05 mg/L: Permissible only if no alternative
  • Above 0.05 mg/L: Not suitable

16. Dissolved Oxygen (DO)

Oxygen dissolved in water; low levels indicate organic pollution.

  • 6 mg/L or more: Healthy
  • 4–6 mg/L: Slightly low
  • Below 4 mg/L: Poor

🚰 Water Use Suggestion

After you click Check Quality on the Water tab, the website tells you which uses the water suits, based on the readings you entered.

  • Drinking and cooking (BIS IS 10500 limits)
  • Bathing and washing clothes
  • Irrigation of crops and gardens
  • Drinking water for cattle and poultry
  • Fish tank or fish farming
  • Boiler and cooling use
  • Mixing concrete for construction

Water Safety Note

These parameters alone cannot confirm that water is safe to drink. Microbiological and chemical testing is also necessary.

🌱 Soil Parameter Guidance

1. Soil pH

Measures the acidity or alkalinity of soil.

  • Below 5: Strongly acidic
  • 5–6: Moderately acidic
  • 6–7.5: Generally suitable for many crops
  • Above 7.5: Alkaline

2. Soil Moisture

Measures the amount of water present in soil.

  • Below 10%: Very dry
  • 10–20%: Low moisture
  • 20–60%: General screening range
  • Above 60%: High moisture

3. Soil Electrical Conductivity (EC)

Measures the concentration of soluble salts in soil.

  • Below 2 dS/m: Generally low salinity
  • 2–4 dS/m: Elevated salinity
  • Above 4 dS/m: Salinity may affect sensitive crops

4. Soil Nitrogen

Nitrogen is an important nutrient required for plant growth.

  • Below 20 mg/kg: Low illustrative value
  • 20–50 mg/kg: Moderate illustrative value
  • Above 50 mg/kg: Relatively high illustrative value

5. Organic Matter

Organic matter consists of decomposed plant and animal materials.

  • Below 1%: Low
  • 1–3%: Moderate
  • 3–5%: Good general reference
  • Above 5%: High

6. Available Phosphorus

Phosphorus supports root development and flowering.

  • Below 10 mg/kg: Low
  • 10–25 mg/kg: Moderate
  • 25–50 mg/kg: Illustrative good range

7. Available Potassium

Potassium helps plants regulate water and supports growth.

  • Below 60 mg/kg: Low
  • 60–120 mg/kg: Moderate
  • 120–250 mg/kg: Illustrative good range

8. Soil Temperature

Measures the temperature of soil around plant roots.

  • Below 5°C: Very cold
  • 5–15°C: Cool
  • 15–30°C: Generally favourable
  • 30–40°C: Warm to hot
  • Above 40°C: Very hot

🌾 Crop Suggestion

After you click Check Quality on the Soil tab, the website compares your readings with the needs of common crops and lists the most suitable ones with a match percentage. Enter at least Soil pH (more readings give better suggestions).

  • 80% or more: Highly suitable
  • 60–79%: Suitable with some care or soil improvement
  • Soil pH and salinity (EC) matter most; low nutrients can be corrected with fertiliser or compost

Soil Testing Note

Soil reference values vary with soil type, crop, climate and laboratory testing method. Follow local agricultural soil-test recommendations.
